Approach

How I think
about this work

Principle 01

Culture is logic, not just behaviour

Most cross-cultural friction isn't about manners or miscommunication — it's about fundamentally different frameworks for how decisions should be made, how trust is built, and what leadership is supposed to look like. I work at that level.

Principle 02

Good questions change rooms

The most powerful facilitation tool isn't an agenda or a framework — it's the right question at the right moment. My philosophy training taught me to find the assumption hiding underneath a stated position. That skill is just as valuable in a boardroom as a seminar room.

Principle 03

Real understanding precedes real change

Coaching that produces lasting results starts with genuine comprehension — of the person, their context, and the constraints they're actually operating within. I don't apply frameworks; I help people build their own.

Principle 04

Complexity is not an obstacle — it's the material

The situations that most need a coach or facilitator are the ones that resist easy answers. I'm drawn to complexity, and my background across cultures, educational systems, and business environments means I can hold nuance without flattening it.
